STRATES

3D Hex with gravity and hidden caverns — a turn-by-turn duel: two players on the same screen, against the AI, or online in a LOB7 room.

The board

A 4×4 base; each column holds up to 4 cubes, always filled from the ground, with no holes (strict gravity). Each player has 16 cubes in reserve; the board starts empty. Azur starts.

One action per turn

1. PLACE — a cube from your reserve on top of a column (if height < 4). Click a ground cell, or the top of a column.

2. MOVE — one of your cubes to an adjacent column (±x or ±y): it lands on top. Climb of at most 1 level (destination top ≤ cube height); free descent. The origin column compresses: whatever was above falls back. Click your cube (side face) then a coppery ghost.

3. MINE — remove one of your cubes, even a buried one: it returns to your reserve and the column compresses one notch — enemy cubes fall. A tactical weapon. Double-click your cube, press M, or use the “Mine” button.

Victory — vertical Hex

Azur links the West face (x=0) to the East face (x=3); Corail links South (y=0) to North (y=3), through a chain of face-touching cubes — adjacency in 6 directions (±x, ±y, ±z). Checked after every action, collapses included. Simultaneous connections: the active player wins.

Occlusion — the heart of the game

Each player sees the board from their own side, with a fixed isometric camera: Azur from the West, Corail from the South. A low cube hidden behind a tall column is invisible: ambush is legal. No free rotation. Between turns, the “Pass the device” screen protects both views.

Tomography

Every 8 half-turns, one random horizontal layer (z = 0 to 3) goes into X-ray for the whole turn: its cubes — even occluded — appear as a translucent see-through overlay. Public and momentary.

Minimap

Top right: the grid seen from above with the physical height of each column — public information. Colors, however, can only be seen from your own side.

Game modes

Same screen — the original duel, with a “pass the device” screen between turns. Against the AI — you are Azur, the machine is Corail: Easy plays about 60 % random legal moves, Normal greedily scores every legal action (connect, block, preserve). Online — LOB7 room — two browsers joined in a shared lob7.com room.

The AI is honest

It knows its own cubes everywhere, but only takes Azur’s cubes into account when they are visible from its camera (the South) or revealed by a tomography scan during its turn — never a masked position. It therefore sometimes reasons over a poorer board than the real one: that is the price of honesty.

Online — honesty note

The smallest identifier becomes Azur and hosts the refereeing: only that browser holds the complete volume, validates moves and never broadcasts the raw state — Corail only ever receives its own cubes, the enemy cubes visible from the South, the public heights and the active tomography scan. The host remains a technical referee: their browser physically knows the full state, so discretion is in order — but nothing invisible is ever sent to it, and victory is proclaimed by the host alone.

Why impossible before computers: fair occlusion — each player only sees from their own side — requires a screen; on a physical model, one cheats by changing the angle.
